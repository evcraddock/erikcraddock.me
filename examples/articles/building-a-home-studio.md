# Building a Home Studio on a Budget

When I decided to start recording my own music, I didn't have thousands of dollars to spend on a professional studio. What I did have was a spare closet, some determination, and a willingness to learn.

## The Space

The first challenge was finding a suitable space. My apartment isn't large, but I discovered that a walk-in closet could work surprisingly well. The small size actually helps with sound isolation, and the clothes act as natural sound absorption.

## Essential Gear

Here's what I started with:

- **Audio Interface**: Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 (~$170)
- **Microphone**: Audio-Technica AT2020 (~$100)
- **Headphones**: Audio-Technica ATH-M50x (~$150)
- **DAW**: Reaper ($60 license)

Total initial investment: under $500.

## Acoustic Treatment

Rather than buying expensive acoustic panels, I made my own using:

- Rockwool insulation from the hardware store
- Simple wooden frames
- Breathable fabric to cover

Four panels cost me about $80 in materials.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Start simple** - You don't need everything at once
2. **Room treatment matters** - More than expensive gear
3. **Learn your tools** - A cheap mic in skilled hands beats expensive gear used poorly

The studio has grown over time, but those early recordings taught me more than any gear upgrade ever could.
