# Writing Your First Song: A Practical Guide

You don't need to be a virtuoso to write a song. You don't need expensive equipment. You don't need permission. You just need to start.

## The Four Chord Song

Here's a secret the music industry doesn't advertise: thousands of hit songs use the same four chords. In the key of C:

**C - G - Am - F**

Play these in order. Congratulations, you have a song structure. Now you just need a melody and words.

## Finding a Melody

Hum over your chord progression. Don't think too hard. Let your voice wander until something feels right. Record it on your phone—you'll forget it otherwise.

Melodies often follow predictable patterns:

- Rising through the verse
- Peaking at the chorus
- Falling during resolution

Don't aim for originality. Aim for something that feels natural to sing.

## Writing Lyrics

Start with what you know. First songs don't need to be profound. Write about:

- A specific moment or memory
- An emotion you're feeling
- A story someone told you
- A question you're wrestling with

### Structure

Most songs follow:

```
Verse 1 (set the scene)
Chorus (the hook, the main idea)
Verse 2 (develop the story)
Chorus
Bridge (new perspective)
Chorus
```

### Tips

- **Show, don't tell**: "Empty coffee cup on the counter" beats "I felt lonely"
- **Use concrete images**: Specific details resonate more than abstractions
- **Imperfect rhymes are fine**: "Home" and "alone" work well enough

## Your First Draft

Set a timer for 30 minutes. Write a complete song, even if it's terrible. You can't edit a blank page.

The goal isn't perfection. The goal is completion. A finished bad song teaches more than an unfinished masterpiece.

## What Next?

1. Record a rough demo on your phone
2. Let it sit for a day
3. Listen with fresh ears
4. Revise one element at a time
5. Share it with one trusted person

Then write another song. And another. The first ten will probably be rough. That's fine. You're learning.

Everyone's first song is awkward. Write it anyway.
