# Learning Guitar at 40

"You're too old to learn guitar." I heard this from well-meaning friends when I bought my first acoustic at forty. Two years later, I'm playing songs I love and wondering why I waited so long.

## The Myth of the Young Learner

Yes, children learn certain things faster. But adults have advantages too:

- **Discipline** - We can commit to consistent practice
- **Context** - We understand music theory more readily
- **Motivation** - We know exactly why we want to learn
- **Resources** - We can invest in quality instruction

## My Approach

I tried learning from YouTube videos for three months. Progress was slow and frustrating. Then I hired a teacher for bi-weekly lessons. Everything changed.

A good teacher:

- Corrects bad habits before they solidify
- Provides structured progression
- Keeps you accountable
- Adjusts to your goals and pace

## The First Year

Month 1-3: Basic chords (G, C, D, Em, Am). Painful finger tips. Questionable rhythm.

Month 4-6: Simple songs. Transitions getting smoother. First time playing something recognizable.

Month 7-12: Barre chords. The F chord nearly broke me. Perseverance won.

## What I'd Do Differently

1. **Start with a better guitar** - Cheap guitars are harder to play
2. **Get a teacher immediately** - Self-teaching created habits I had to unlearn
3. **Practice shorter sessions** - 20 minutes daily beats 2 hours weekly
4. **Record myself** - Hearing playback reveals issues you miss in the moment

## The Joy

Last month, I played "Wish You Were Here" around a campfire with friends. Nothing fancy. Just chords and singing. It was one of the most satisfying moments of my adult life.

Start today. Your future self will thank you.
